Principle 1

Inherent value

Nearly every metric waits for someone else to act. A paper is worth nothing until cited; a dataset, nothing until downloaded. That makes the metric a measure of luck and visibility as much as of contribution.

The floor refuses that. A dataset prepared properly, documented, licensed and deposited where others can find it is already a contribution. It is worth at least 1, today, whether or not anyone has come looking for it.

Principle 3

Disciplinary norms and ethics

A climate dataset might be downloaded ten thousand times. A rare-disease cohort might be downloaded forty times, by exactly the forty people who can use it, and be the more valuable of the two. Treating those numbers as comparable is a category error.

So every score is divided by what is normal in its own field. Without this term, the S-index would reward your choice of field over the quality of your work.

Principle 2 Principle 4

What a dataset is worth, and why we can trust it

Sraw opens into 0.1·θ + ω. Downloads, plus citations weighted ten times more heavily, because reuse in the literature is the stronger signal.

But those numbers only mean something if the dataset qualifies first. A dataset with no persistent identifier cannot be cited at all, so ω would not exist. A dataset with no contributor records offers no way to exclude your own downloads, so θ could be inflated at will.

The gate is not an obstacle in front of the equation. It is the reason the equation means anything.
Every dataset must clear all three:

A proposal for the NIH data sharing challenge

Sharing data is a contribution. Nothing measures it.

The h-index counts papers. It cannot see the datasets a researcher shares, the people who reuse them, or the care taken to make them reusable at all. The S-index is built for that work, from four principles rather than from whatever metadata happened to be available.

Where the h-index falls short

Six problems we designed against

Every one is a known failure of citation counting. A data-sharing metric that inherits them has solved nothing.

Field dependenceA 20 in oncology is not a 20 in mathematics.
Narrow scopeOnly published papers count. Data and curation do not.
Blind to rolesThe person who curated the data and the person who signed off score alike.
Citations as valueBeing cited and being useful are not the same thing.
GameableSelf-citation inflates it, and everyone knows it.
No ethics gateRigour, consent and governance are invisible to it.
The foundation

Four principles, decided before any maths

We began by asking what a data-sharing metric must reward. Only then did we build one that does. Each principle became a specific piece of the calculation, and each keeps its colour everywhere it appears.

1
Inherent value

FAIR-aligned sharing is a real contribution, even when nobody has reused the data yet.

→ the floor
2
How & where shared

Whether data can be reused at all depends on where it lives and how it is described.

→ the gate
3
Norms & ethics

Impact means different things in different fields, so it should be judged inside one.

→ normalisation
4
Collaborative credit

Research is collective. Contributions across the data lifecycle should be named.

→ ORCID + CRediT
